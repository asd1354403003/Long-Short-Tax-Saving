References: three AQR research reports

The reading path separates the alpha model, portfolio design and tax mechanism. The following notes summarize the papers; the implementation sections that follow document this project's own choices.

R1 · 2023 · MODEL & ACCOUNT DESIGN

Beyond Direct Indexing: Dynamic Direct Long-Short Investing

Connects a stock-selection model to long/short account design and tax-aware implementation. Appendix A is the starting reference for the original value, momentum and quality model. The paper also studies changing leverage and tracking error over time.

R2 · 2023 · PORTFOLIO CHOICES

Key Design Choices in Long/Short Equity

Explains systematic versus discretionary selection, single-stock shorts versus an index hedge, and how managers combine signals. Its discussion of funding and cash returns motivates separating a stock-selection spread from a fully funded account.

Original PDF · pages 4, 6-9

R3 · 2024 · TAX MECHANISM

Loss Harvesting or Gain Deferral? A Surprising Source of Tax Benefits of Tax-Aware Long-Short Strategies

Examines why tax-aware long/short strategies can realize net losses while following an alpha model. It emphasizes gain deferral alongside loss realization through natural portfolio turnover. This supplies the research basis for step 3, which remains unimplemented here.

Original PDF · findings and turnover analysis

The reference alpha model in the AQR report

R1 ranks each characteristic within industries, centers and standardizes those ranks, removes market beta, and scales the three factor portfolios by predicted volatility. It combines them equally and derives stock-level alphas using a lagged covariance model. Its account optimization then adds implementation and tax considerations. R1, Appendix A.

The strategy presented here is Market Neutral. This project uses historical IWB sectors, price-based risk estimates and a tuned fixed blend. The cited reports inform the research; the implementation, parameter selection and reported results belong to this project.

R2 discusses the information in both sides' stock selection and the associated shorting costs. The 50/50 exposure used here follows this project's gross and net constraints rather than a fixed allocation prescribed by the reference reports. R2, short-side discussion.

What is treated as known at a decision?

  1. Use financials whose effective available_from is on or before the completed month-end cutoff. Retain known report/reference availability dates.
  2. If no date is recorded, assume availability at fiscal period end plus 90 calendar days. This rule applies only to missing dates and is labeled as an assumption.
  3. Build TTM totals only from four consecutive available fiscal quarters. Do not skip a missing quarter or insert an annual/YTD number as a standalone quarter.
  4. Keep signed earnings, cash flow and book equity. Positive denominators are required for the ratios that use assets or market capitalization.
  5. Use historical market capitalization, not today's market cap or valuation multiple. No current ratio is filled back into older decisions.
Point-in-time limits: downloaded financials are latest vendor versions and can contain restatements. A date reference alone does not prove the value was the originally published value. The original release times of historical IWB snapshots and Yahoo share counts are not independently verified. A 2021-12-31 decision must not use a 2021 annual report published in 2022. A +90-day assumption improves separation from period-end but does not establish a verified historical filing timestamp.

Primary source links: iShares IWB; Yahoo Finance; SEC EDGAR. The source history and limitations above are from the project's preserved files, not claims of complete historical coverage by these services.

Step 1 · implemented

Select candidates at each rebalance

Start with that month's active IWB proxy members, valid historical prices and market cap, the recorded US/USD source filters and a known historical sector. For each small factor combination, drop the ticker-date row if one of its required inputs is missing or nonfinite. Require at least ten eligible stocks in a ranking group. A ticker excluded this month can return in a later month.

There is no requirement that a stock have complete inputs for the entire 2022-2026 period. The selected model does not require every stock to have all ten factors in the union below: each leaf requires its own subset. Historical beta, and volatility when used for sizing, must also be available.

From raw characteristic to score

3. Calculate the mean and absolute sum explicitly

The zero mean is a result of standardization, not an assumption about all alpha scores. For each factor, z = (rank - mean rank) / rank standard deviation, so its z-scores sum to zero. The same four stocks and fixed factor weights are used for both factors: mean(score) = 0.60 × mean(z_CF) + 0.40 × mean(z_quality) = 0.60 × 0 + 0.40 × 0 = 0. Tiny floating-point residuals can remain, which is why the code still demeans. The denominator 2.8622 is this example's absolute sum, not a parameter or a constant.

Combined scores (more digits): [0.626099, 0.804984, -0.804984, -0.626099] Mean = (0.626099 + 0.804984 - 0.804984 - 0.626099) / 4 = 0 Centered scores = scores - 0 = the same four scores Absolute sum = |0.626099| + |0.804984| + |-0.804984| + |-0.626099| = 0.626099 + 0.804984 + 0.804984 + 0.626099 = 2.862167... ≈ 2.8622 (using unrounded scores) Stock A base weight = 0.626099... / 2.862167... = 21.875% Long weights: 21.875% + 28.125% = 50% Short weights: -28.125% - 21.875% = -50%

Values are displayed rounded; weights use unrounded calculations. Four stocks keep the arithmetic short; production ranking requires at least ten eligible stocks per group. This illustrates base weights only, before tail selection, beta adjustments, portfolio blending and the 5% single-name cap.

For choice 1, alpha_column selects an existing dated input. Each component can retain score tails, apply a score-magnitude power, and scale positions by inverse volatility. Sector budgets can follow signal strength, equal allocation or group size. The saved Python preset contains the complete construction settings.

initial magnitude(i) = abs(base_weight(i)) ** power / volatility(i) ** inverse_vol within a sector: normalize positive and negative magnitudes to equal dollar budgets beta direction(i) = estimated_beta(i) - mean beta of selected names in the sector beta adjustment = w - [(w dot beta) / (beta_direction dot beta_direction)] * beta_direction gross normalization: w / sum_i abs(w_i)

Historical beta and volatility use a trailing 252-session price-return window, ending at the decision date; beta needs at least 200 paired market observations. Beta estimates use matching observed stock/SPY dates. Some children smooth only current and past targets over two months, reapply current eligibility and restore the risk controls. No later target is used.

Why long +50% and short -50%?

net = sum(w_i) = L - S = 0 gross = sum(abs(w_i)) = L + S = 1 therefore L = 0.5, S = 0.5

This is a split of NAV dollar exposure, not a requirement to own the same number of long and short stocks. The targets average 41.2 long names and 35.3 short names. Estimated beta neutrality is an additional constraint: dollar neutrality alone does not guarantee beta=0.

Combine the component portfolios

The selected model combines factor portfolios and sector filters using allocations fixed across years. Blend the component targets, normalize the netted result to gross=1, and restore sector and beta neutrality after each blending step. A sector filter keeps its specified sector before rebuilding risk-neutral targets. The final projection preserves selected signs while enforcing long +0.5, short -0.5, zero sector net exposure, zero estimated beta and a 5% single-name limit. Infeasible selections raise rather than relax these constraints.

The paper's market-neutral alpha sleeve can be incorporated into an account with a different net exposure, such as a long-biased extension account. This project currently tests the standalone gross=1 neutral sleeve, without the benchmark overlay or tax-aware optimization described in R1. R1, account construction.

Opening NAV includes held positions marked at execution Opens where available. Integer rounding can leave residual cash and small departures from target constraints. A missing Open defers the relevant order; a missing held Close carries its previous mark and records a stale-price flag. This selected result has zero stale-price legs. Splits adjust quantities and cost basis; fractional cash-in-lieu uses the recorded settlement logic.

The 1% annual borrow rate is an assumed common rate, not a historical security-specific locate quote. Each session accrues on closing short market value using ACT/360; intervening non-trading days accrue on the prior close. Transaction costs apply to actual traded notional on both buys and sells, including trims and sign flips. There is no extra terminal liquidation fee: final open positions are marked to market, with unrealized P&L included.

NAV excludes dividends, short dividend compensation, cash interest, taxes and additional financing/locate constraints. An execution-price Open is an idealized fill assumption; no separate spread/slippage model is added beyond the recorded 5 bps. Average-cost bookkeeping is for P&L attribution and is not a tax-lot model.

Risk-free rates, averages and Sharpe

DGS1MO supplies daily observations of a one-month Treasury yield quoted as an annual percentage. It is neither a monthly return nor a realized daily Treasury-index return. FRED series definition.

Historical quote mean
4.0349% annualized, averaging 1,201 valid daily quotes over January 1, 2022 to October 22, 2026. No monthly averaging file is used.
Mean aligned RF return
0.01609% per NAV interval, including calendar-day gaps over weekends and holidays.
Selected calculation
Match historical RF to each interval, form daily excess returns, then take their mean and sample standard deviation. This is the calculation behind 1.6364.
Cash in NAV
No interest credit. The RF benchmark is deducted for Sharpe; it does not change the account's displayed P&L.
q(t) = latest valid annual percent quote strictly before the NAV interval starts d(t) = calendar days in the NAV interval RF(t) = [1 + q(t) / 100 / 365] ** d(t) - 1 excess(t) = net_return(t) - RF(t) Sharpe = sqrt(252) * mean(excess) / sample_std(excess), ddof=1 realized beta = covariance(net_return, SPY_price_return) / variance(SPY_price_return)

The first Open-to-Close NAV interval is approximated by one day for RF accrual. Quotes older than seven days, absent coverage or invalid values raise. Historical yields are used rather than today's RF applied to all years; no missing date is filled with zero or a future quote.

Resolution of the earlier “average RF” discussion: the mean annual quote is reported above, but the saved model uses historical daily interval RF. The averaging inside its Sharpe is the average of daily excess returns. Calling 1.6364 a fixed-average-RF result would misdescribe the stored calculation.
Average-rate comparison using the same saved NAV

If the full-period annual quote mean (4.0349%) is instead converted to one constant trading-day return using (1 + average_annual_yield) ** (1/252) - 1, the same NAV produces Sharpe 1.6544. This is a descriptive sensitivity calculation, not the benchmark used to select or label the saved result. No trades or parameters were rerun.

RF provenance and checksum are preserved in DGS1MO.metadata.json. The timestamp is UTC; its October 27 UTC retrieval time falls on October 26 in New York. The RF proxy's cumulative growth over the aligned period is 21.41%.

Why only a small fixed pool?Use the full 2022-2026 window and the changing historical monthly IWB proxy. Eligibility is recomputed immediately before each scheduled rebalance for the factors required by that model.

Step 3 · research only

Tax-loss harvesting and gain deferral

R3 explains its tax-aware results through deferring gains while continuing to trade loss positions and open new positions as the alpha model changes. Most turnover still moves toward the alpha model. Realized net capital loss is therefore a tax-accounting outcome, not a claim that the investment account lost the same amount economically. R3, findings; turnover analysis.

R1 also studies changing leverage and tracking error without immediately realizing all deferred gains; fully unwinding an extension can release previously deferred gains. These are results under the papers' simulation assumptions, not the output of this project's current model. R1, overview.

What a later implementation would need

  • Security-level tax lots, acquisition dates, adjusted basis, holding periods and explicit lot-disposal rules.
  • Separate realized gains, realized losses, unrealized gains, taxes paid and deferred tax liabilities; after-tax liquidation comparisons.
  • Investor-specific tax assumptions and applicable trading restrictions, including wash-sale treatment and short-sale rules verified for that implementation.
  • A tax-aware trade objective balancing alpha retention, risk, trading and borrow costs against tax consequences; compare with the identical tax-agnostic portfolio.
Current status: the account has an average-cost P&L ledger, but no tax lots, tax-rate model, wash-sale engine, harvesting rule or after-tax return. The 91.66% and Sharpe 1.6364 are net of modeled trading/borrow costs, before tax. No tax benefit is included.
